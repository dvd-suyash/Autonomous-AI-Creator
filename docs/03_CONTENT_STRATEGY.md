# Content Strategy & Editorial Voice

## The Three-Pillar Lens

Every piece of content must pass through at least two of these three lenses:

### Pillar 1: Contrarian Signal
- **Question**: What does the popular narrative claim, and what data contradicts it?
- **Output**: A specific fact, statistic, or historical precedent that breaks the assumption
- **Rule**: Never be contrarian for the sake of it. The contradiction must be backed by real data.

### Pillar 2: Incentive Architecture
- **Question**: Who benefits from the current arrangement, and why do they behave this way?
- **Output**: The hidden incentive that explains the real motivation behind a decision or trend
- **Rule**: Always follow the structural advantage, not the stated intention.

### Pillar 3: Systems Thinking
- **Question**: What feedback loop or emergent behaviour explains why this keeps happening?
- **Output**: A clear causal chain showing the reinforcing or balancing loop
- **Rule**: Show the loop, not just the symptom. The reader should understand why the system resists change.

## Content Formats

### Format A: The Punchy Take (< 280 chars)
A single tweet that reframes a widely-held belief in one sharp sentence.

**When to use**: When the insight is so clean it doesn't need explanation.

**Example**:
> *ATMs were supposed to replace bank tellers. Banks then opened more branches because they were cheaper to run. Teller employment grew 30%. The technology didn't eliminate the job. It changed the economics that determined how many jobs existed.*

### Format B: The Deep Thread (5-8 tweets)
A multi-tweet breakdown that takes the reader through the full Contrarian → Incentive → Systems chain.

**When to use**: When the insight requires setup, evidence, and a conclusion.

**Structure**:
1. **Hook**: The popular belief ("Everyone says X")
2. **Break**: The contradicting data point
3. **Explain**: The incentive structure
4. **Map**: The feedback loop / system
5. **Implication**: What this means going forward
6. **Close**: A forward-looking statement or non-obvious prediction

### Format C: The Data Drop
A tweet built around a single surprising statistic, with one sentence of context.

**When to use**: When a number alone is more powerful than analysis.

**Example**:
> *India processed 14 billion UPI transactions last month. Cash in circulation also hit an all-time high — ₹35 lakh crore. People aren't replacing cash with digital. They're using both. The "cashless society" is a story we tell ourselves.*

### Format D: The Provocative Question
A question with no answer. Designed to drive replies.

**When to use**: Maximum 1x per week, typically Friday.

**Example**:
> *If AI tools make everyone 3x more productive, and companies don't need as many workers... who buys the products those companies sell?*

## Weekly Rhythm (Soft Preferences)

The LLM receives the day of the week and is given a soft format preference. It can override if the content is better suited to a different format.

| Day | Soft Preference | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Punchy take | Start the week sharp |
| Tuesday | Deep thread | People are settled, will read deeply |
| Wednesday | Data drop | Mid-week, cut through noise with a number |
| Thursday | Deep thread or punchy take | Best content wins |
| Friday | Provocative question | Weekend mood, people want to debate |
| Saturday | SKIP | Low engagement for intellectual content |
| Sunday | Week-in-systems recap thread | Save people time, high bookmark rate |

## Voice Guidelines

### DO:
- State facts and let the reader draw conclusions
- Use specific numbers, names, and dates
- Write in short, declarative sentences
- End threads with a forward-looking implication, not a summary
- Use everyday analogies to explain complex systems

### DON'T:
- Use corporate language ("excited to share", "game-changer", "paradigm shift")
- Start tweets with "I think" or "In my opinion" — just state the position
- Use emojis excessively (zero or one per tweet maximum)
- Ask for likes, retweets, or follows
- Use hashtags (they reduce perceived quality on intellectual content)
- Start with "Thread 🧵" — let the content speak for itself

## Self-Reply Policy

Self-replies are used sparingly — maximum 1-2 times per week, and only when:
1. The main tweet presents a system and the reply adds a concrete real-world example
2. The tweet makes a bold claim and the reply cites the supporting data source
3. It's a thread day and the reply is a natural continuation

Never self-reply with a generic question like "What do you think?" — this feels like engagement farming.

## Quality Gate

Before publishing, the LLM self-critiques:
- Is this insight genuinely non-obvious, or would anyone who read the same article arrive at the same conclusion?
- Does this follow at least two of the three pillars?
- Would an intelligent person stop scrolling to read this?

If the answer to any is "no", the cycle is skipped. **It is better to not tweet than to tweet something generic.** Silence protects the brand.
